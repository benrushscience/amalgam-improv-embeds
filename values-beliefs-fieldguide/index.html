A Field Guide to Beliefs and Values

In improv, beliefs and values give your character reasons to act, react, and disagree. They help you make choices from the character’s point of view as a scene unfolds. A value is something a character considers important, such as fairness, family, or freedom. A belief is an idea they accept as true or hold about how things ought to be, such as “People can change” or “Everyone should follow the same rules.” Browse the Values or Beliefs index, open a family, or search for an idea. Choose an entry, try its scene setup and character line, and follow related entries to develop the character’s point of view.

How to use an entry

Let your scene partner’s offers test the character’s convictions. Notice when two motivations conflict, and let the character surprise you rather than treating an entry as a fixed personality type.

Example scenarios and lines offers three independent situations with a line for each. Pick one to explore, adapt it to your scene, and respond to your partner’s choices.
